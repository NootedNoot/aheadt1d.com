🚨 When to get help right away

Call 911

  • Passed out, having a seizure, or too out of it to swallow safely.
  • Don't put food or drink in their mouth. Give emergency glucagon if you have it, and roll them onto their side.

Call the care team or go to the ER

  • Throwing up, bad stomach pain, breathing fast and deep, or fruity-smelling breath.
  • Ketones are moderate or high.

These are signs of DKA. It can get serious fast, so don't wait it out.

Your care team's own plan always wins. Keep their after-hours number somewhere easy to find.

🧭 The basics, in plain English

🔑

What Type 1 actually is

The immune system attacked the cells in the pancreas that make insulin. Insulin is the key that lets sugar out of the blood and into the body's cells. No key = sugar piles up in the blood while the body runs short on fuel. So insulin has to come from shots or a pump now. Nobody caused this. Not food, not anything you did.

⬇️

Lows (under 70)

Too much insulin, not enough food, or lots of activity. You might see shaky, sweaty, pale, cranky, hungry, or confused. Fix it with the 15-15 rule: 15 grams of fast sugar (glucose tabs, juice, regular soda), wait 15 minutes, check again, and repeat if still under 70. Little kids may need less, so ask your team what amount to use.

⬆️

Highs & ketones

One high number isn't an emergency. Being high for hours, or being sick, is when you check ketones. Ketones mean the body is burning fat because it can't use sugar. Lots of them turns into DKA. Never skip background (basal) insulin, even if they're sick and not eating.

Go deeper: ketones & DKA →
📱

Why the sensor and the finger stick don't match

A CGM reads the fluid under the skin, not blood, so it runs a few minutes behind. That gap is biggest when sugar is moving fast. If the number doesn't match how they feel, do a finger stick. Sleeping on the sensor can also fake a low.

Go deeper: sensor lag & stacking insulin →
🌱

The long game

You'll hear about eyes, kidneys, and nerves. That damage comes from years of high numbers, not one bad day or even a bad week. Keeping numbers in range most of the time lowers that risk a lot. Aim for progress. Nobody's perfect at this.
