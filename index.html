How it fits together

Each app does one job.

A reading travels from your sensor to your alerts — and, if you choose, to the people who care about you.

  1. Dexcom G7

    Sends a reading about every 5 minutes.

  2. Bluetooth

  3. AheadBLE

    Talks to the sensor and hands every reading to Ahead.

  4. Health Connect
    + live ping

  5. Ahead

    Analyzes on your phone, projects ahead, raises alerts.

  6. Optional
    sharing

  7. Ahead Lite

    On family’s phones, via your Ahead account. Calm alerts.

Who needs which account?

The main account

Ahead: the person with Type 1

They sign up in Ahead with their own email. This account holds their glucose and decides who can follow. Ahead should always be signed in as the person living with Type 1, never as a follower.

Companion accounts

Ahead Lite: one per follower

Each family member or caregiver signs up in Ahead Lite on their own phone, with their own email: one account per person, per device. One Lite account can follow several people, like a parent with two kids.

No account

AheadBLE: nothing to sign up for

It only connects your sensor to your phone. The person with Type 1 sets it up once, on the phone that wears the sensor.

The main account shares to companion accounts from Menu → Account → Manage Sharing in Ahead. Companions need a verified email first. More in the FAQ.

Why the order matters: Ahead doesn’t read your sensor itself — it reads Health Connect. If AheadBLE isn’t running, Ahead sees an empty feed. That’s why AheadBLE goes first. The dashed part is optional: Ahead works fully on its own without sharing with anyone.

Setup guide

Install it in a few minutes.

Tick steps off as you go — your progress is remembered on this device.

0 of 7 done

  1. Download AheadBLE and allow the install

    On the phone that will wear the sensor. When Android asks, allow your browser to install apps (Settings → Install unknown apps). You can switch that back off afterwards.

    Play Protect may say the app is unrecognized. That’s normal for a beta shared outside the store. Only continue if you got it from this page and the fingerprint matches.

  2. Pair your G7 in AheadBLE

    Open AheadBLE (it appears as Ahead Connect) and allow the Bluetooth (“Nearby devices”), notification and Health Connect permissions. Enter the 4-digit pairing code from your G7 applicator — or scan its barcode — and start monitoring. Keep the phone close to the sensor.

  3. Wait for “Sensor connected :)”

    AheadBLE’s notification tells you the truth. Don’t move on until you see it.

    Brand-new sensor? The G7’s own warm-up (about 30 minutes) means no readings yet. That’s normal — the notification flips once they start.

  4. Keep AheadBLE alive

    Set its battery to Unrestricted (Settings → Apps → Ahead Connect → Battery) so Android doesn’t put it to sleep overnight.

    Tip: on Samsung (and some other phones) also lock the app in Recents. In our own daily use it seems to help the app survive everything without hurting battery much.

  5. Install Ahead

    Create your account (or sign in) and follow the setup wizard. This is the main account — sign up as the person living with Type 1. Let Health Connect share blood glucose with it, and allow notifications, exact alarms and Do Not Disturb access so urgent alerts can break through. Battery to Unrestricted, and lock it in Recents too.

  6. Check the loop is closed

    Within a few minutes Ahead should show your current number and trend. If it says there’s no data, go back to step 3 — AheadBLE has to show connected first.

  7. Share with family Optional

    In Ahead open Menu → Account → Manage Sharing, type their email and tap Grant access. They need an Ahead Lite account with a verified email first.

You’re all set. AheadBLE is connected and Ahead is watching. You don’t need to keep opening either one. Next new sensor? See how to switch sensors.

0 of 6 done

  1. Download Ahead Lite and allow the install

    On your own phone. When Android asks, allow your browser to install apps (Settings → Install unknown apps). You can turn that back off afterwards.

    Play Protect may say the app is unrecognized — normal for a beta shared outside the store. Only continue if it came from this page and the fingerprint matches.

  2. Create your account and verify your email

    This is your own companion account — one per person, per phone. Sign up with the email address the person will share with, then tap the link in the verification email — sharing won’t work until your email is verified. Check spam if it doesn’t arrive.

  3. Ask them to share with you

    This part is theirs to do. In Ahead they open Menu → Account → Manage Sharing, type your email and tap Grant access. You can’t do it for them — that’s deliberate, so nobody can look at someone’s glucose without being invited.

  4. Pick who you’re following

    Ahead Lite lists whoever has shared with you (if there’s only one, it opens automatically). Not there yet? Open Account in the menu — the list refreshes live — or sign out and back in.

  5. Let it reach you

    Allow notifications and set the app’s battery to Unrestricted (Settings → Apps → Ahead Lite → Battery) so alerts arrive while your phone sleeps. You can also have alerts spoken under Alerts & Voice Settings.

    Tip: on Samsung (and some other phones) also lock the app in Recents so Android doesn’t close it.

  6. Know what you’re looking at

    You’ll see their current number, trend, a 15-minute projection and a 3-hour chart. These are estimates from glucose alone — they don’t know about insulin, food or activity. If you’re worried, call them.

    Your sign-in lasts about 30 days, so open the app about once a month. If it ever says it can’t reach data, open it and sign in again.

You’re all set. You’ll get a calm heads-up if they’re dropping or low, or if their data goes quiet.

The part everyone asks about

What is AheadBLE, and why does Ahead need it?

Ahead never talks to your sensor directly. It reads your glucose from Android’s Health Connect. AheadBLE is the small app whose only job is to talk to your Dexcom G7 over Bluetooth and put every reading where Ahead can use it.

Set AheadBLE up first. Ahead has nothing to show without it.

Install it, pair your sensor, and wait until its notification says “Sensor connected :)” before you open Ahead. It’s a quick, one-time step per sensor — then you can forget about it.

What it does

  • Pairs with your Dexcom G7 using the 4-digit code on the applicator.
  • Receives each reading over Bluetooth (about every 5 minutes).
  • Saves it to Health Connect and sends Ahead a live ping, so alerts never wait on a timer.
  • Fills in readings it missed once it reconnects.

It heals itself

  • Runs as a foreground service (that’s its persistent notification) so Android keeps it alive.
  • If the link drops it keeps scanning, even with the screen off, and reconnects on its own.
  • Backup alarms and a start-up receiver restart it after a reboot or app update.
  • Its status is honest: it never says “connected” when readings have stopped.

Honest limits

  • Dexcom G7 only. Not G6, Libre or other sensors.
  • Android only, with Bluetooth on and the phone reasonably near the sensor.
  • Let AheadBLE be the only app connecting to the sensor. Running the official Dexcom app alongside it is untested.
  • Beta software — keep another way of being alerted.

What happens when the connection dropsNothing for you to do — but here’s what to expect

  1. The link drops

    Bluetooth hiccups, you walk away from the phone, or the sensor sleeps between transmissions.

  2. AheadBLE keeps looking

    It scans continuously. The G7 only wakes to talk about every 5 minutes, so a reconnect can take up to one cycle.

  3. It reconnects and catches up

    Once it hears the sensor it reconnects, then pulls any readings it missed. If a saved connection goes stale, it forgets it and finds the sensor again.

  4. The status stays honest

    If no fresh reading has arrived for about 12 minutes, the notification flips to “disconnected”, then back once readings resume.

Got a new sensor? Open AheadBLE, use Pair a different sensor (or the one-tap New sensor reset) and enter the new 4-digit code. Ahead itself needs no changes. More in the FAQ.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Search, filter by topic, or open everything. Every answer has a link you can copy to send to someone.

Which apps do I need?

If you live with Type 1 and wear a Dexcom G7: install AheadBLE first, then Ahead.

If you’re a parent, partner or friend following someone: install Ahead Lite. They share their stream with you. You never need AheadBLE or Ahead on a follower’s phone.

Getting started
How do accounts work? Who needs what?

The person with Type 1 has the main account. They sign up in Ahead with their own email, and that account holds their glucose. Ahead should always be signed in with their account — it’s the source everything else follows.

Everyone who follows gets their own companion account. Each family member or caregiver installs Ahead Lite on their own phone and signs up there with their own email — one Lite account per person, per device. They verify their email, then the person with Type 1 shares with them from Menu → Account → Manage Sharing.

One Lite account can follow several people. A parent with two kids, for example, has one Lite account and both kids share with it. But Ahead itself is only ever for the person living with Type 1 — don’t sign into Ahead with a family member’s account to watch someone; that’s what Ahead Lite is for.

AheadBLE has no account at all. It only connects your sensor to your phone.

Getting started
Why isn’t it on the Play Store?

Ahead is an early beta built by one person, and it isn’t on the Play Store yet. Sharing the APK directly gets fixes to people quickly. The trade-off is that Android asks you to allow installs from your browser and Play Protect may warn you — which is why every file has a published fingerprint.

Getting started
Is it safe to install an APK from a website?

Only install APKs from this page, and check the file with the fingerprint checker first. If it doesn’t match, delete it. After installing, you can switch “Install unknown apps” back off for your browser. To see exactly what each app asks for, read Permissions.

Getting started
Does it work on iPhone?

No — AheadBLE, Ahead and Ahead Lite are all Android-only for now.

Getting started
Which Android versions are supported?

Android 9 or newer, and the apps are built for Android 15. AheadBLE and Ahead use Health Connect, which is built into Android 14 and up; on Android 9–13 it’s a free app from Google Play. Ahead Lite doesn’t need it.

Getting started
What is AheadBLE?

AheadBLE (it appears on your phone as Ahead Connect) is a small app with one job: talk to your Dexcom G7 over Bluetooth and hand each reading to Ahead. It saves every reading to Health Connect and also pings Ahead instantly, so alerts don’t wait on a timer. More on AheadBLE.

AheadBLE
Does AheadBLE need an account?

No. AheadBLE has no sign-in and doesn’t use the internet — it just pairs with your sensor and passes readings to your phone. Your account lives in Ahead. How accounts work.

AheadBLE
Does Ahead work without AheadBLE?

Ahead needs a live glucose source. It doesn’t connect to the sensor itself — it reads Health Connect. AheadBLE is the app that fills Health Connect from a G7, and it’s the one we build and test Ahead with. Until AheadBLE is installed, connected and showing “Sensor connected :)”, Ahead has nothing to show.

Other apps that write to Health Connect could feed Ahead too, but that route isn’t covered or tested here.

AheadBLE
Which sensors work?

AheadBLE is built for the Dexcom G7. It doesn’t support the G6, FreeStyle Libre or other sensors.

AheadBLE
Where do I find the 4-digit pairing code?

It’s printed on your G7 applicator — check the box too if you can’t spot it. You can type it in or scan the applicator’s barcode with your camera. Keep the applicator handy until you’ve paired.

AheadBLE
What does “self-healing” mean?

AheadBLE is built to look after itself so you don’t have to keep opening it:

  • It runs as a foreground service (that’s its persistent notification), which is how Android knows to keep it alive.
  • If the Bluetooth link drops, it keeps scanning for your sensor — even with the screen off — and reconnects on its own the next time the sensor wakes up (about every 5 minutes).
  • After reconnecting it pulls any readings it missed.
  • Backup alarms and a start-up receiver restart it after a reboot or an app update.
  • If a saved connection goes stale, it forgets it and finds the sensor again instead of getting stuck.
  • Its notification is honest: Sensor connected :) when a fresh reading arrived in the last ~12 minutes, Sensor disconnected :( otherwise.
AheadBLE
How do I switch to a new sensor?

Each G7 has its own pairing code. When you start a new one, open AheadBLE, choose Pair a different sensor (or the one-tap New sensor reset, which clears the old sensor’s details), and enter the new 4-digit code. Ahead itself needs no changes.

AheadBLE
Can I use the Dexcom app at the same time?

That’s untested. We recommend AheadBLE be the only app that connects directly to your sensor. Treat running both as unsupported.

AheadBLE
Why does AheadBLE ask for Location?

Android 11 and older require the Location permission before an app can scan for Bluetooth devices. AheadBLE uses it only for that scan and never reads where you are. On newer Android versions it asks only for “Nearby devices”.

AheadBLE
What does Ahead do differently from my CGM app?

A normal CGM app tells you what your number is. Ahead also looks at how fast it’s moving and projects 15 and 30 minutes ahead, so it can nudge you before you cross a line. Yellow warnings play a distinct tone; red alerts use vibration and spoken voice. It also has Alarm Thresholds — your own alerts for a value or a rate of change.

Ahead
What are Alarm Thresholds?

Found in Ahead’s menu under Safety → Alarm Thresholds. They’re tripwires you set yourself — “alert me if I climb to this number” or “if I’m falling faster than this rate.” Make one permanent, or “just for today.”

When a threshold is crossed it sends one alert that can sound even through Ahead’s Silence and Do Not Disturb, then goes quiet again unless things get meaningfully worse or it’s crossed again later.

Ahead
Will alerts get through Do Not Disturb or when my phone is asleep?

Red alerts and Alarm Thresholds are set up to break through Do Not Disturb once you grant Ahead “Do Not Disturb access”. But Android’s battery management can still delay or stop background apps, so set AheadBLE and Ahead to Unrestricted battery and lock them in Recents. Treat alerts as best-effort, never guaranteed.

Ahead
Does Ahead work offline?

Ahead does its analysis and alerting on your phone, so alerts don’t need a connection. You need internet to sign in, sync to your account and share with family. AheadBLE only needs Bluetooth.

Ahead
What is Ahead Lite — is it a stripped-down Ahead?

No. Ahead Lite is a companion for the people who care about you, not a lighter version for the person with diabetes. It shows their current number, trend, a 15-minute projection and a 3-hour chart, alerts on lows and fast drops, and tells you calmly if their data goes quiet. It never touches Bluetooth or Health Connect.

Ahead Lite

Can I follow more than one person in Ahead Lite?

Each person shares with your email from their own Ahead app, and Ahead Lite lists everyone who has shared with you. Pick who you’re looking at from that list. (If only one person has shared, it opens straight to them.)

How do I delete my account?

In Ahead or Ahead Lite go to Menu → Account → Delete account and confirm with your password. That deletes your account and revokes its access. If you’d like confirmation that your stored readings are gone too, email hello@aheadt1d.com.

Safety & privacy
Tip: lock the app in Recents so Android doesn’t close it

This one comes from our own daily use on a Samsung phone: locking the app in Recents seems to help it survive everything, without costing much battery.

  1. Tap the Recents button — the one to the right of the Home button — to see your open apps.
  2. Tap the app’s icon at the top of its card. A small menu with a few options appears.
  3. Choose Lock this app (on some versions it reads Keep open).

Do it for AheadBLE (Ahead Connect) and Ahead — and Ahead Lite if you follow someone. A padlock appears on the card, which keeps the app out of the way of the “Close all” button and the phone’s automatic clean-up.

Not every phone has this: Samsung does, and some other brands have a similar “lock” option in Recents. If you don’t see it, don’t worry — the Unrestricted battery setting matters more. Locking is a helper, not a guarantee.

Troubleshooting
AheadBLE says “Sensor disconnected :(” — what do I do?

Give it a few minutes first: the G7 only transmits about every 5 minutes, so a reconnect can take a moment. If it’s still disconnected after about 15 minutes:

  1. Check Bluetooth is on and the phone is near the sensor.
  2. Check AheadBLE’s battery is Unrestricted and that it hasn’t been force-stopped. Locking it in Recents helps too.
  3. Open AheadBLE and confirm monitoring is running.
  4. Just started a new sensor? Use Pair a different sensor and enter the new code (see switching sensors).

Still stuck? Email hello@aheadt1d.com.

Troubleshooting
Ahead says “No new data” or shows nothing

Ahead reads from Health Connect, which AheadBLE fills. So: (1) check AheadBLE shows Sensor connected :); (2) check Ahead still has Health Connect permission to read blood glucose; (3) give it a few minutes — a new reading only arrives about every 5 minutes; (4) if you just started a new sensor, remember the G7’s ~30-minute warm-up.

Troubleshooting
Notifications are late or missing

Almost always Android’s battery management. Check, for each app: notifications are allowed; battery is set to Unrestricted; the app isn’t on any “sleeping apps” or “battery optimization” list (Samsung and some other makers have their own); and, for Ahead, that Do Not Disturb access is granted. Locking the app in Recents can help too.

Troubleshooting
How do I update to a newer version?

Download the new APK from this page and open it — Android installs it over the old one and keeps your data, as long as it’s the same app from this page. Check the version on the card and verify the fingerprint.

If Android refuses because the app “conflicts with an existing package”, uninstall the old version first. Your Ahead account and cloud data are safe; you’ll just sign in again.

Troubleshooting
How do I uninstall?

Long-press the app and choose Uninstall, like any other app. Readings AheadBLE already saved stay in Health Connect until you delete them there.
